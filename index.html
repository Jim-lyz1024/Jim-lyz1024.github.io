My PhD is part of ReWildID, an MBIE Smart Ideas project at the University of Auckland that develops AI to re-identify individual animals from camera-trap images. I focus on stoats, the elusive introduced predators that threaten native wildlife across Aotearoa New Zealand.

Most of my work is on animal re-identification (ReID). I'm interested in how vision-language models, multimodal context and generative models can make it work reliably on real camera-trap data, where lighting, pose and background change all the time. In MetaWild, for example, we paired 20,890 camera-trap images with environmental metadata such as temperature and whether it was day or night, and showed that ReID models do better when they can use it. I also help build the project's toolkit, so that conservation practitioners can run these models on their own images.

Before my PhD I did an MSc in Computer Science at Auckland, with a thesis on domain generalisation using vision-language models, and a BEng in Computer Science at Northeast Forestry University in Harbin, China.

If you work with camera-trap data or on animal ReID, I'd be glad to hear from you. Email: yil708 [at] aucklanduni.ac.nz

  1. ECCV 2026

    Multiple Images Distract Large Multimodal Models via Attention Fragmentation

    Tingrui Qiao, Di Zhao, Yuzhuo Li, Bo Pang, Caroline Walker, Chris Cunningham, Yun Sing Koh

    European Conference on Computer Vision (ECCV), 2026

    Background tokens in each image act as attention sinks, and earlier images collect more of that sink attention, so multi-image LMMs spread their focus instead of finding the key evidence. We call this attention fragmentation.

  2. WACV 2026

    Overcoming Fine-Grained Visual Challenges in Animal Re-Identification via Semantic Feature Alignment

    Yihao Wu, Di Zhao, Yuzhuo Li, Matthew Alajas, Alistair S. Glen, Jingfeng Zhang, Gillian Dobbie, Daniel Wilson, Yun Sing Koh

    IEEE/CVF Winter Conference on Applications of Computer Vision (WACV), 2026

    CARE generates image-conditioned text descriptions and aligns them with visual features. This targets the core difficulty of animal ReID: the same individual can look very different, while different individuals can look almost the same.

    20,890 camera-trap images of six species, each paired with environmental metadata. We also propose the Meta-Feature Adapter (MFA), which lets vision-language ReID models use that metadata and improves on images alone.
